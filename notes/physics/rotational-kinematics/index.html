rotational kinematics

rigid systems, angular displacement, angular velocity, radians, and the rotational kinematic equations
study mode

Rotation of a rigid system

A system is in translational motion when every part moves together in the same direction, and in rotational motion when it turns around an axis of rotation. Many objects do both at once: a skateboard wheel translates down the street while spinning about its axle.

Picture a playground merry-go-round. Its axis of rotation runs through the center, perpendicular to the deck, and it is a rigid system: it holds its shape while it spins. A rider at the rim races along a big circle while a rider near the center creeps along a small one, yet in any time interval both sweep through the same angle. That shared angle is what the angular quantities track.

Linear vs. angular view of one spinning body

Linear velocity \(\vec{v}\) tangent to the point's circle, so it differs from point to point
Distance covered in \(\Delta t\) depends on \(r\) grows with distance from the axis
Angle swept in \(\Delta t\) same for every point the whole body turns together
Why "rigid" matters if points swept different angles, the body would stretch or bunch up

Because every point has a different linear velocity, a rotating body cannot be modeled as a single point object. Angular quantities describe the one motion the whole system shares.

Angular velocity and acceleration

The angle a rotating body sweeps through is its angular displacement \(\Delta\class{var-theta}{\theta}\), with a direction given as clockwise or counterclockwise. The average angular velocity is the angular displacement divided by the time it takes:

\[\class{var-omega}{\omega_\text{avg}} = \dfrac{\Delta \class{var-theta}{\theta}}{\Delta t}\]

If the spin rate changes, the body has an angular acceleration, the rate at which angular velocity changes:

\[\class{var-alpha}{\alpha_\text{avg}} = \dfrac{\Delta \class{var-omega}{\omega}}{\Delta t}\]

Variables

\(\Delta\theta\) angular displacement (rad)
\(\omega\) angular velocity (rad/s)
\(\alpha\) angular acceleration (rad/s²)
\(\Delta t\) elapsed time (s)

Key properties

Shared by all points every point on a rigid body has the same \(\omega\) and \(\alpha\)
Sign convention counterclockwise positive clockwise negative
Constant spin rate \(\omega\) constant, \(\alpha = 0\)
Speeding up or slowing \(\alpha \neq 0\), same sign as \(\omega\) to speed up, opposite to slow down

These two definitions mirror \(v_\text{avg} = \Delta x / \Delta t\) and \(a_\text{avg} = \Delta v / \Delta t\) exactly. Rotational kinematics is translational kinematics with angles in place of positions.

Radians

Pick any point a distance \(r\) from the axis. One full turn carries it around a circumference \(2\pi r\), so the ratio of distance traveled to radius is \(2\pi\) no matter which point you pick. That radius-independent ratio is the radian measure of the angle:

\[\class{var-theta}{\theta} = \dfrac{s}{r} \qquad 1\ \text{revolution} = 360^\circ = 2\pi\ \text{rad}\]

Common conversions

Quarter revolution \(\pi/2\ \text{rad} = 90^\circ\)
Half revolution \(\pi\ \text{rad} = 180^\circ\)
Full revolution \(2\pi\ \text{rad} \approx 6.28\ \text{rad}\)
\(N\) revolutions \(2\pi N\ \text{rad}\), keep counting past \(2\pi\), don't reset

Worked example: merry-go-round

Given \(3.5\) counterclockwise revolutions in \(14\ \text{s}\)
Angular displacement \(\Delta\theta = 3.5 \times 2\pi = 7\pi \approx 22\ \text{rad}\)
Average angular velocity \(\omega_\text{avg} = \dfrac{7\pi}{14} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.6\ \text{rad/s}\)
Direction counterclockwise, so both are positive

A radian is a ratio of two lengths, so it is dimensionless. That is what lets it convert cleanly between angular and linear quantities in the next section.

Connecting linear and rotational motion

Rearranging \(\theta = s/r\) gives the arc length a point travels during a rotation:

\[\Delta s = r\,\Delta\class{var-theta}{\theta}\]

Divide both sides by \(\Delta t\) and the same pattern links the rates:

\[v = r\class{var-omega}{\omega} \qquad a_\text{tan} = r\class{var-alpha}{\alpha}\]

Reading the r-scaling

\(r\) distance from the axis of rotation (m)
\(v = r\omega\) linear speed, tangent to the circle; angles must be in radians
\(a_\text{tan} = r\alpha\) tangential component only, from speeding up or slowing down
Centripetal component \(a_c = v^2/r\) comes from turning, not from \(\alpha\); it exists even at constant \(\omega\)

Every rider on the merry-go-round shares the same \(\omega\), but \(v = r\class{var-omega}{\omega}\) grows with radius. That is why the rim feels fast and the center feels calm: same spin, more meters of circle per radian.

Rotational kinematic equations

For constant angular acceleration, the angular quantities obey the same algebra as the linear kinematic equations, with \(x \to \theta\), \(v \to \omega\), and \(a \to \alpha\):

\[\begin{aligned}&\class{var-omega}{\omega} = \omega_0 + \class{var-alpha}{\alpha} t\\&\Delta\class{var-theta}{\theta} = \omega_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}\class{var-alpha}{\alpha} t^2\\&\class{var-omega}{\omega}^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\class{var-alpha}{\alpha}\,\Delta\class{var-theta}{\theta}\end{aligned}\]

Linear to rotational dictionary

\(\Delta x \to \Delta\theta\) displacement becomes angle swept
\(v \to \omega\) velocity becomes angular velocity
\(a \to \alpha\) acceleration becomes angular acceleration
Signs choose counterclockwise as positive and keep it consistent

Worked example: ceiling fan

Given spinning at \(\omega_0 = 12\ \text{rad/s}\), switched off, stops in \(8\ \text{s}\)
Angular acceleration \(0 = 12 + \alpha(8) \implies \alpha = -1.5\ \text{rad/s}^2\)
Sign check negative \(\alpha\) opposes the positive spin, so it slows
Angle before stopping \(\Delta\theta = 12(8) + \tfrac{1}{2}(-1.5)(8)^2 = 48\ \text{rad} \approx 7.6\ \text{rev}\)

Every strategy from linear kinematics carries over: list the knowns, pick the equation missing the variable you don't need, and let the signs track direction.

Key takeaways

Summary

Rigid system holds its shape; every point shares the same \(\Delta\theta\), \(\omega\), and \(\alpha\)
Angular velocity \(\class{var-omega}{\omega_\text{avg}} = \Delta\class{var-theta}{\theta}/\Delta t\), in rad/s
Angular acceleration \(\class{var-alpha}{\alpha_\text{avg}} = \Delta\class{var-omega}{\omega}/\Delta t\), in rad/s²
Radians \(\theta = s/r\); 1 revolution \(= 360^\circ = 2\pi\) rad
Linear connections \(\Delta s = r\Delta\class{var-theta}{\theta}\), \(v = r\class{var-omega}{\omega}\), \(a_\text{tan} = r\class{var-alpha}{\alpha}\)
Tangential vs. centripetal \(a_\text{tan} = r\alpha\) changes speed; \(a_c = v^2/r\) changes direction
Kinematic equations same three as linear motion with \(\theta\), \(\omega\), \(\alpha\); counterclockwise positive
