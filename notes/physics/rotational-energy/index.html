rotational energy

rotational kinetic energy, total kinetic energy of a rigid system, and rolling without slipping
study mode

Rotational kinetic energy

A spinning object has kinetic energy even if it goes nowhere, because every piece of its mass is moving in a circle. Summing \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\) over all those pieces gives the rotational kinetic energy, written in terms of the rigid system's rotational inertia and angular velocity:

\[\class{var-k}{K_\text{rot}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\class{var-inertia}{I}\class{var-omega}{\omega}^2\]

Variables

\(K_\text{rot}\) rotational kinetic energy (J)
\(I\) rotational inertia about the axis (kg·m²)
\(\omega\) angular velocity (rad/s)

Key properties

Scalar no direction; spin sense does not matter since \(\omega\) is squared always ≥ 0
Same currency measured in joules; trades freely with \(U_g\), \(U_s\), and translational \(K\)
Grows with \(\omega^2\) doubling the spin rate quadruples the stored energy
Mirrors \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2\) \(m \to I\), \(v \to \omega\): the usual rotational dictionary

This is not a new kind of energy, just ordinary kinetic energy tallied for circular motion. Grid-scale flywheels exploit the \(\omega^2\) growth: spin a heavy disk fast in a vacuum chamber and it banks megajoules that can be drawn back out as electricity seconds later.

Total kinetic energy

A rigid system can translate and rotate at the same time, and its total kinetic energy splits cleanly into two pieces: translational kinetic energy from the linear motion of its center of mass, plus rotational kinetic energy from its rotation about the center of mass:

\[\class{var-k}{K_\text{total}} = \tfrac{1}{2}M\class{var-v}{v_\text{cm}}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\class{var-inertia}{I_\text{cm}}\class{var-omega}{\omega}^2\]

The two contributions

\(\tfrac{1}{2}Mv_\text{cm}^2\) translational: total mass \(M\) moving at the center-of-mass speed
\(\tfrac{1}{2}I_\text{cm}\omega^2\) rotational: spin about the center of mass, using \(I\) about that axis

Three kinds of motion

Pure translation \(\omega = 0\): a crate sliding on ice, only \(\tfrac{1}{2}Mv^2\) translational
Pure rotation \(v_\text{cm} = 0\): a ceiling fan, only \(\tfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2\) rotational
Both at once a rolling wheel or tumbling gymnast carries the sum add them

Picture a drone propeller that snaps off mid-flight: it sails across the yard while spinning furiously. Its center of mass follows an ordinary projectile path carrying \(\tfrac{1}{2}Mv_\text{cm}^2\), and the blur of rotation about that point carries \(\tfrac{1}{2}I_\text{cm}\omega^2\) on top.

Rolling without slipping

A wheel rolls without slipping when it turns without skidding or spinning in place: the contact point grips the ground, so each moment it is momentarily at rest. That grip locks the translation to the rotation:

\[\class{var-v}{v_\text{cm}} = R\class{var-omega}{\omega}\]

The rolling constraint

Links the two motions \(v_\text{cm} = R\omega\) and \(a_\text{cm} = R\alpha\); one unknown, not two
Held by static friction the contact point does not slide, so it is static friction, not kinetic
Friction does no work it acts on a point that is not moving energy conserved
Slipping breaks it a skidding tire has \(v_\text{cm} \neq R\omega\) and kinetic friction burns energy energy lost

Point speeds on a rolling wheel

Top of the wheel \(2v_\text{cm}\): translation and spin add
Center \(v_\text{cm}\): pure translation
Contact point zero: translation and spin cancel momentarily at rest

This is why the bottom of a rolling bike tire looks sharp in a photo while the top blurs: the top really is moving twice as fast as the bike. When a problem says "rolls without slipping," it is handing you the constraint \(v_\text{cm} = R\omega\) and permission to conserve energy.

Energy conservation with rolling

Since static friction does no work, a ball rolling down a hill conserves mechanical energy. The potential energy it gives up must feed both kinetic terms, with the constraint \(\omega = v_\text{cm}/R\) tying them together:

\[Mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}M\class{var-v}{v_\text{cm}}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\class{var-inertia}{I_\text{cm}}\class{var-omega}{\omega}^2\]

Solving a rolling problem

1. Write the energy bar \(U_g\) at the top \(=\) translational \(+\) rotational \(K\) at the bottom
2. Apply the constraint replace \(\omega\) with \(v_\text{cm}/R\); the \(R\)'s cancel
3. Use the shape write \(I = cMR^2\) (sphere \(c = \tfrac{2}{5}\), disk \(\tfrac{1}{2}\), hoop \(1\))
4. Solve \(v_\text{cm} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2gh}{1 + c}}\); mass and radius drop out

Worked example: bowling ball ramp

Given a \(6.0\ \text{kg}\) bowling ball (solid sphere) rolls from rest down a \(1.4\ \text{m}\) tall ramp
Energy \(Mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}Mv^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{2}{5}MR^2\right)\left(\tfrac{v}{R}\right)^2 = \tfrac{7}{10}Mv^2\)
Speed \(v = \sqrt{\tfrac{10}{7}gh} = \sqrt{\tfrac{10}{7}(9.8)(1.4)} \approx 4.4\ \text{m/s}\)
Compare sliding friction-free it would hit \(\sqrt{2gh} \approx 5.2\ \text{m/s}\) rolling is slower

Race a hoop, a disk, and a sphere down the same ramp and the sphere wins, disk second, hoop last, no matter their masses or radii. The larger the shape factor \(c\), the bigger the slice of \(Mgh\) locked up in spin instead of forward motion. Nothing beats a frictionless sliding block, which puts every joule into translation.

Key takeaways

Summary

Rotational kinetic energy \(\class{var-k}{K_\text{rot}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\class{var-inertia}{I}\class{var-omega}{\omega}^2\), a scalar in joules; never negative
Total kinetic energy \(\class{var-k}{K} = \tfrac{1}{2}M\class{var-v}{v_\text{cm}}^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\class{var-inertia}{I_\text{cm}}\class{var-omega}{\omega}^2\): center-of-mass translation plus rotation about it
Rolling constraint rolling without slipping locks \(\class{var-v}{v_\text{cm}} = R\class{var-omega}{\omega}\) and \(a_\text{cm} = R\alpha\)
Static friction in rolling acts at a momentarily-at-rest contact point, so it does no work; energy is conserved
Ramp speed with \(I = cMR^2\), \(v_\text{cm} = \sqrt{2gh/(1+c)}\); mass and radius cancel
Rolling race sphere beats disk beats hoop; a frictionless slider beats them all
